Hardware 101

Understand the 15 key components inside your computer. What they do and why they matter.

Core Components

CPU (Processor)

The brain of the computer. Executes instructions and performs calculations. Speed is measured in GHz.

GPU (Graphics Card)

Handles rendering images, video, and games. Essential for gaming, video editing, and 3D work.

RAM (Memory)

Short-term storage for running programs. More RAM lets you multitask smoothly. Measured in GB.

Storage (HDD/SSD)

Long-term storage for files, OS, and programs. SSDs are faster but pricier than HDDs.

Motherboard

The main circuit board that connects all components together. Determines compatibility.

Power Supply (PSU)

Converts wall power to usable voltages for components. Wattage rating indicates capacity.

Peripherals & Extras

Cooling System

Fans, heatsinks, or liquid cooling keep components from overheating. Essential for performance.

Case

The enclosure that houses everything. Comes in various sizes (ATX, micro-ATX, mini-ITX).

Monitor

Displays visuals. Key specs: resolution, refresh rate, response time, panel type.

Keyboard

Input device for typing. Mechanical or membrane. Key rollover and switches matter for gamers.

Mouse

Pointing device. Optical/laser sensors, DPI settings, and ergonomics affect precision and comfort.

Network Card / Wi-Fi Adapter

Connects to internet. Ethernet for stability, Wi-Fi for convenience. Speeds vary (Wi-Fi 5, 6, 6E).

Sound Card / Audio

Processes audio output/input. Most motherboards have built-in; dedicated cards for audiophiles.

Optical Drive

Reads/writes CDs, DVDs, Blu-rays. Less common now, but still used for media and backups.

Ports & Connectors

USB, HDMI, DisplayPort, Thunderbolt, etc. Determine what peripherals you can connect.
